U.S. machinery imports by country of origin

Trade disruption changed the economics—and the map—of machinery sourcing.

National import data show two connected changes: the effective tariff burden rose abruptly, while an expanding U.S. machinery market shifted away from China.

The effective tariff rate equals calculated duties divided by customs value. It is a trade-weighted realized measure, so it reflects statutory duties, product mix, origin mix, and applicable tariff programs. Monthly values are not seasonally adjusted. The pattern identifies a changed cost environment; it does not establish how any individual company responded.

Structural reconfiguration · 2017–2025

The market expanded. China lost ground.

U.S. imports in the four selected machinery families increased by 81.1%. Imports from China declined by 32.4%. Demand for imported equipment continued to grow, but the geography supplying it changed.

About the data

National evidence, interpreted at the appropriate scale

Imports for consumption for HTS 8421, 8483, 8501, and 8504 were aggregated across all U.S. customs districts and organized by country of origin. The tariff chart uses monthly observations from January 2024 through July 2026. The structural chart uses complete calendar years from 2017 through 2025.

These results describe the U.S. import environment facing companies. They do not represent Greater Cincinnati or Northern Kentucky trade flows and do not identify the behavior of individual firms.
